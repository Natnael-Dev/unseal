# Unseal product, publication and pilot handbook

Dexter approved the recommendations in the 35-question decision record on 24 November 2026. This document describes intended behavior. Deployment, learner feedback, publication and pilot outcomes need their own dated evidence.

## 1 · Product brief

A student finds a useful internship guide through a club link. They can inspect a sample while alumni and peers sponsor the finished guide. Once the target is reached and the creator releases it, anyone can read and save the same verified edition, including a learner who contributed nothing. Unseal's broader direction includes other kinds of knowledge and creative work. One English-language resource for Ethiopian university students anchors the MVP.

## 2 · Requirements and acceptance

| ID | User need | Observable acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Decide whether a guide is worth supporting | A visitor without a wallet can inspect its publisher, audience, license, contents, sample, target, deadlines, mint and devnet notice. |
| P2 | Understand what a contribution buys | Review shows chosen amount, remaining gap, network costs, designated creator, public release promise and both refund conditions before a signature. A sponsor receives a recoverable receipt. |
| P3 | Reach the exact target together | Only accepted on-chain contributions count. The UI rereads remaining capacity after a conflicting pledge. Smaller final remainder is fundable. |
| P4 | Release as soon as fully funded | Funding closes at target. Creator can submit the release immediately. Reader marks it public after finalized release and successful verification. |
| P5 | Recover from an unsuccessful campaign | After applicable expiry or authorized cancellation, each sponsor can independently reclaim their recorded contribution once. A refresh restores status. |
| P6 | Read independently | A new, unsigned visitor opens the released edition on a separate reader origin, verifies it and saves it. Reopening after disconnecting succeeds on a device with the required saved files. |
| P7 | Publish responsibly | Invited publisher submits original finished work, preview, rights and attribution. Before sponsorship opens, operator checks readable mobile layout, mirror bytes and matching published terms. |

## 3 · People and jobs

| Person | Job and constraint | Product response |
|---|---|---|
| Ethiopian university student applying for a first internship | Turn limited experience into an honest application using a phone and intermittent connectivity | Concrete examples, small self-contained pack, public preview, wallet-free reading and explicit offline save. |
| University alumnus or student-club sponsor | Help many learners with a flexible contribution and understand the risk before signing | Suggested amounts, exact remaining gap, immutable payout terms, discoverable receipt, optional recognition. |
| Invited creator, initially Dexter | Prepare useful work in advance, receive payment on release, explain authorship | Creator checklist, file verification, deadline reminders in the interface, fixed recipient and honest credit. |
| Curator/operator | Select and check pilot content, handle a rights or availability report | Limited intake, manual checklist and dated incident notes. Hosting and curation controls have different powers from the on-chain program. |

## 4 · Problem and evidence

The working hypothesis is that alumni and clubs will fund a concrete, inspectable resource once for public access, and learners value keeping a low-bandwidth copy. A technical demonstration can establish custody, verification and independent access. It cannot establish demand, learning outcomes or willingness to contribute real funds. All three are **UNVERIFIED** until observed. A founder-written guide establishes authorship only. Do not describe the founder as an independent pilot participant.

Use consented feedback sessions. Ask a student to select a posting, find the matching checklist item and identify one action they would take. Ask how they normally get such guidance, which example is confusing and whether saving the guide helps. Record date, audience category, task, observable outcome, anonymized comment and permission for quotations. Report the denominator including incomplete sessions. Never invent feedback or equate a wallet count with a person.

## 5 · Value and comparison

An ordinary shared guide already serves readers well once someone has paid for its creation. Unseal adds a visible pledge target before publication, constrained payout/refund rules and independent verification of the released edition. A membership or preorder commonly centers the paying member. Here the defining outcome is an unrelated reader opening the same edition. Collective funding for open publication has precedents including [Unglue.it](https://unglue.it/). Ground originality in the actual Solana implementation and its demonstrated reader and refund flows. Avoid first-ever claims.

## 6 · Scope and exclusions

Ship one invited English publication, one campaign per demo scenario, a direct campaign link and a small curated home. Support browser use on phones and desktops, one known classic SPL test mint, flexible contributions, an independent reader, offline save and pull refunds. Use a separate campaign for the refund demonstration. Keep explicit labels wherever examples use devnet funds. Future possibilities include more resource types, additional languages, PDF export and wider publishing. Discovery ranking, identity verification, exclusive access, adjudication of content quality, real-money sponsorship and native apps are outside this release.

## 7 · Success measures

| Measure | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Release works | On a devnet campaign, two separate sponsors reach the target. Record finalized references, vault changes and fixed-payee amount. |
| Public good is delivered | A person or fresh browser that never pledged opens the verified edition from independent reader, saves it, then reads offline. Record what had to be cached. |
| Recovery works | Separate campaign shows a valid refund, changed balances, and rejection of a repeated claim. Verify each supported eligibility branch in tests. |
| Guide is useful | Seek actual student feedback. Record sessions completed, tasks attempted and corrections with permission. Participation and demand stay **UNVERIFIED** until documented. |
| Pilot has interest | Log concrete opt-in requests from creators or sponsor groups separately from page views, demo testers and simulated transactions. External participation is an opportunity. |
| Judges can inspect it | Deliver reproducible repository, actual program address, live links, short video and traceable evidence against each published criterion in the submission plan. |

## 9 · Demo publication brief

**Your first internship application** is an English guide for a university student preparing their first application. The promised outcome is a tailored application draft and a completed pre-send check. The guide may use invented applicant examples clearly marked as illustrative. Avoid representing any example employer as hiring or endorsing the pack.

The preview pairs the finished cover, contents and a short complete worked example. Illustrate a marked-up posting, an evidence-based CV bullet and a final attachment check using original simple drawings with text descriptions. Let learners complete every activity using paper or a phone note. Dexter owns the final editorial review and confirms the finished downloadable edition, preview and license before campaign creation.

## 10 · Guide outline and assets

| Section | Content delivered | Learner task |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Read the opportunity | Annotate role, required qualifications, dates, submission channel and requested files on an expressly fictional posting. | List three requirements and flag one uncertainty for the employer. |
| 2. Find evidence | Map classwork, volunteering, projects and responsibilities to requirements. | Create a two-column requirement/evidence list with honest examples. |
| 3. Tailor a one-page CV | A fictional example changes “worked on a team project” to “organized survey responses in a spreadsheet for a four-person class project.” Explain where each detail came from. | Write one bullet and remove anything the applicant cannot substantiate. |
| 4. Write the approach | Show a short subject line and email naming the role, one relevant example and the attached materials. | Draft a short introduction using their own experience. |
| 5. Submit and track | Explain file naming, delivery channel, timezone checks and local tracking of the submission. | Use the checklist below, then note what was sent and when. |

**Learner pre-send checklist**

- I copied the role title, submission channel and deadline from the actual posting.
- I checked eligibility and used examples that accurately describe my own work.
- I addressed the intended recipient and removed all template placeholders.
- My contact details work, each requested attachment opens, and filenames identify the document.
- I followed the posting's file type, subject line and length instructions.
- I kept a copy and wrote down the date and any follow-up instructions.

**Asset acceptance.** Provide title, edition/date, author/publisher credit, license and attribution text in the pack itself. Keep original illustration files and provenance. Verify alt text, meaningful reading order, no dependence on color, usable mobile type and intact links. Compare the public preview with the exact edition packaged. Record actual measurements of pack size and offline save at rehearsal. This outline is editorial context. The final illustrated publication and its reviewer sign-off are separate deliverables.

## 11 · Authorship, licensing and corrections

Dexter checks each text and illustration for original authorship or documented permission compatible with the publication license. Record item, author, source, permission, edition and attribution. Prefer original art and fictional examples. Obtain separate consent for personal quotations, photographs or names. Link to external sources without implying ownership. Identify external material and its terms explicitly. Unresolved rights block publication of the affected asset.

Suggested credit once confirmed: “Your first internship application, by Dexter (Natnael), edition [date]. Licensed under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).” Substitute the actual published author credit and date. Reusers should credit the creator, link the license and indicate changes. Keep MIT software notices outside the publication attribution. A correction receives a dated new edition with its own identity. Keep prior campaign records inspectable. Existing copies can continue circulating.

## 12 · Publication and sponsorship policy

1. Curator invites a publisher. Publisher supplies a complete pack, contact for pilot coordination, public display name, short introduction, optional profile and rights statement.
2. Curator reviews rights, preview and actual resource against the guide checklist. Publisher confirms the mint, fixed payee, target, file identity, funding deadline and later release deadline before creating the campaign. Show the amount in labeled test units.
3. Sponsor reviews the real excerpt, terms, amount, fees and permanence of their public wallet activity. Validate positive whole base units and show the exact remaining amount. Suggested amounts never silently change a signed request. If another pledge fills capacity, refresh and ask for a new signature only if another amount fits.
4. The final accepted contribution closes funding. Creator can release immediately before the fixed cutoff. During this interval the guide remains awaiting release. Finalized release pays the fixed creator and enables verified public reading.
5. Creator cancellation during funding before its cutoff or while fully funded before the release cutoff makes the campaign immediately refundable. An underfunded campaign at its funding cutoff and a funded campaign unreleased at its release cutoff become refundable. At the cutoff the relevant expired action is closed. Finalized program state determines the outcome of races. A sponsor requests their own contribution back to their bound wallet destination and pays transaction costs in the normal flow. Repeat claims cannot pay twice.
6. A completed release closes refunds. Contact the curator about quality or rights concerns. Curator can correct descriptive metadata or remove a listing, while published terms, earlier releases, already distributed copies and valid claims keep their recorded history.

## 13 · Trust and editorial operations

| Report | Owner and next action | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| Rights or attribution dispute | Curator records URL, claimed issue, date and owner privately, temporarily delists when warranted, asks publisher for provenance and records disposition. | Public listing can be removed or corrected. Existing funding and refund rules still apply. |
| Wrong, damaged or unavailable file | Operator checks both mirrors and expected identity, restores exact bytes if available, opens a dated incident if neither works. | Reader rejects a mismatch and offers a verified alternative or retained local copy. |
| Creator misses deadline | Operator confirms chain time and status. No discretionary payout or pooled refund. | Each eligible sponsor sees a claim action independently. |
| Suspect display name or sponsor recognition | Curator removes disputed optional recognition pending verification. | Wallet activity remains publicly inspectable. |
| Contact unstaffed or broad public intake requested | Dexter must verify a monitored contact and ownership before expansion. | Use invited pilot channels until a real contact is confirmed. |

Report promises according to what is technically true. A hash identifies bytes. It cannot certify usefulness, originality or exclusive possession. The creator can share early. Broadcast release material can reach observers even when a transaction fails. A host, RPC provider or development-network outage can delay access. Disclose who controls program upgrades and hosting before presenting the demo.

### Privacy in the pilot

Read-only visitors need no account, wallet connection or email. The browser saves requested files on the current device and origin. Provide a clear remove-saved-copy control and state that clearing site data or browser eviction may erase it. Keep signing secrets inside the wallet. Never ask people to send seed phrases or private keys. Do not put contacts, feedback, recognition names or an applicant's CV inside chain accounts. The learning tasks happen privately using the student's own tools.

Public wallet addresses, transactions, amounts, timing and campaign links can be correlated. Disclose visibility before signing. Optional names associate an address with a person. Ask consent separately, keep association off chain and support removing the listing. Third parties can retain earlier public information. Document actual host/RPC request logging and provider retention when providers are selected. Avoid analytics by default. Keep pilot feedback minimal, restrict access to the organizer, record any quote permissions and delete identifiable notes once the stated feedback purpose ends. Publish only approved quotes or anonymous aggregate findings. A public project contact is a release dependency for wider use.

### Product risks to carry into delivery

| Risk or early signal | Owner | Control and fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Students find the lesson generic | Dexter, editorial | Test one learner task and revise before packaging. Label missing pilot evidence. |
| No external pilot volunteers | Dexter, outreach | Present honest builder-authored provenance and observed test results. Track expressions of interest separately. |
| Candidate guide embeds personal information or unlicensed art | Publisher and curator | Use fictional examples and original drawings, log rights and remove unresolved assets before funding. |
| Sponsors think full funding automatically publishes the file | Product | Distinct funded/awaiting-release state with absolute deadline and refund explanation. |
| Pending wallet transaction mistaken for success | Engineering | Recover using original signature/receipt, re-read finalized state and prevent duplicate confirmations. |
| Last contribution races, amount rounds, or expired request arrives late | Engineering | Validate base units, cap and chain time on chain. UI explains new gap or closed state. |
| Reveal exposed before payout or bad file survives editorial review | Publisher | Disclose early disclosure risk, verify the exact file and preview in rehearsal, preserve recovery rules. |
| Both mirrors or browser cache are unavailable | Operations | Test independent mirrors, verified local save and offline reload. Show the resulting availability limitation. |
| Devnet, faucet or fees block the demo | Demo lead | Fund test accounts early, retain dated genuine evidence and distinguish recordings from a live attempt. |
| Reach expands beyond support capacity or free quota | Dexter | Limit invited intake, monitor provider limits and verify contact before wider distribution. |
| Deadline compresses verification | Dexter | Prioritize one finished guide, sponsorship, verified independent reading and refunds. Record missed evidence explicitly. |
